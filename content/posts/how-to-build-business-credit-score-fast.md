Most business owners don't even know their business credit score exists until they get turned down for something. A loan, a net-30 account, a lease on commercial space. That's the moment the phone call comes into my office, and I can hear the frustration before they even finish their first sentence.

Here's what I tell them: building business credit is not complicated. But it does require doing a handful of specific things in the right order, with some patience built in. The people who struggle with it are almost always skipping steps or expecting it to work like personal credit. It doesn't.

Let me walk you through what actually works.

---

## First, Separate Your Business From You (This Is Non-Negotiable)

I cannot count how many business owners I've met who've been operating for three, four, five years with zero business credit because everything runs through their personal accounts and their personal Social Security number. All that revenue, all those vendor relationships, none of it building anything they can actually use.

Business credit lives in a separate ecosystem from personal credit. The three main business credit bureaus are Dun & Bradstreet, Experian Business, and Equifax Business. They track your business as its own entity, and for that to work, your business has to actually exist as a separate entity in their systems.

That means you need a few foundational things locked down before anything else will stick:

A registered business entity (LLC or corporation, not just a sole proprietorship operating under your name). A Federal Employer Identification Number (EIN) from the IRS, which you can get free at [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed) in about ten minutes. A dedicated business checking account. A business address that's not just your personal address if you can help it. A business phone number listed under the business name.

That last one trips people up. Dun & Bradstreet in particular verifies business information through directory listings. If your business phone number isn't connected to your business name in a listed directory, it can slow things down. Use a Google Voice number tied to your business name if you need a low-cost option.

And get your D-U-N-S Number. Dun & Bradstreet assigns this for free, and it's your business's primary identifier in their system. Go to dnb.com and request one. It can take up to 30 days, so do it on day one.

## The Fastest Real Path: Vendor Credit That Actually Reports

Here's where most of the advice you'll read online goes sideways. Everyone wants to talk about business credit cards and bank loans. Those matter, but they're not where you start if you're building from nothing or trying to build fast.

You start with vendor accounts that report to the business credit bureaus.

These are called "net-30 accounts" or "trade credit." A vendor extends you credit to purchase goods or services, and you pay the invoice within 30 days. When they report that payment history to Dun & Bradstreet (or Experian Business), it starts building your file. Pay early, not just on time, and it builds faster on D&B because their PAYDEX score rewards early payment with a score of 80 or higher.

The vendors most commonly recommended for starter accounts (because they actually report, and approve new businesses without a credit history) include Uline, Quill, Grainger, and Crown Office Supplies. Uline is probably the most talked-about. You order basic shipping supplies, net-30 terms, they report to D&B. Simple.

A few honest caveats here: not every vendor reports to all three bureaus, and reporting timelines vary. Some vendors take 30 to 90 days to report after a payment. This is normal and it's annoying. You're not doing anything wrong.

Get 3 to 5 of these net-30 accounts open, use them, and pay early or on time every single time. That's your foundation. I've seen businesses go from no credit file to a functioning D&B profile with a solid PAYDEX score in under six months doing exactly this.

## Adding Business Credit Cards the Smart Way

Once you have 3 months or so of trade line history, you're in a better position to apply for a business credit card that reports to business bureaus specifically. This is important: not all business credit cards report to business bureaus. Some report only to personal bureaus, which doesn't help your business credit at all.

Cards from Capital One, American Express, and some credit unions tend to report to business bureaus. Before applying for any business card, call the issuer and ask directly: "Do you report payment history to Dun & Bradstreet, Experian Business, or Equifax Business?" If they say no or they don't know, keep looking.

Keep utilization low. Under 30% is the general guidance, same principle as personal credit. If you have a $5,000 limit, don't carry a balance over $1,500. Pay it off in full monthly if you can. The goal here is payment history and low utilization, not the rewards points (even if those are nice).

One thing I want to address because it comes up constantly: secured business credit cards. If your personal credit is under 650 and you're having trouble getting approved for a standard business card, a secured card is a reasonable bridge. You put down a deposit, typically $200 to $500, and get a credit line in roughly that amount. It's not glamorous, but it works, and the payment history builds the same way.

## The Part Everyone Skips: Monitoring Your Own Files

You will not know if something is wrong unless you check. And things go wrong more often than you'd think, especially in the early stages when you're actively trying to build.

Dun & Bradstreet has a free tier at dnb.com that lets you see a limited version of your profile. Paid plans like D&B Credit Monitor start around $39/month and give you more detail. Nav.com is worth knowing about, it aggregates your business credit scores from multiple bureaus and gives you a dashboard view. Their free plan is genuinely useful. The paid plan, Nav Business Boost, runs about $49.99/month and also has a feature that reports your payments to business bureaus, which is a legitimate accelerator if you're trying to speed things up.

Check your Experian Business and Equifax Business files too. You can get those directly through their business credit portals. Look for errors, unverified trade lines, incorrect addresses. Dispute anything that's wrong. The [U.S. Small Business Administration](https://www.sba.gov/) has solid guidance on business credit monitoring if you want a primer before you start pulling files.

I had a client last year, a three-year-old landscaping company, whose D&B file had an old address that kept preventing vendors from verifying the business. Cost them two net-30 approvals before we figured it out. Simple fix once we found it, but they'd been spinning their wheels for months.

## How Long Does This Actually Take?

Honestly, faster than most people expect if you follow the steps, and slower than the "build business credit in 30 days!" ads promise.

A realistic timeline: 30 to 60 days to get your foundational setup done (EIN, entity, D-U-N-S number, bank account, business phone). Another 3 to 6 months of paying net-30 accounts to build a real payment history. At the 6-month mark with consistent payments, you'll typically have a PAYDEX score in the 70-80 range if you've paid on time, higher if you've paid early. That's enough to start getting approved for better vendor terms and some business credit cards.

A year in, if you've been consistent, you should have a file strong enough for a small business loan or line of credit with a bank or credit union.

Anyone promising you a strong business credit profile in 30 days is either selling you something or leaving out the part where it only works if you already had partial credit built. I'd steer clear of those programs, especially the ones that charge you $500 to "set up your business credit" by doing things you can do yourself for free.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to build a business credit score from scratch?

Most businesses can establish a functioning business credit profile in 3 to 6 months by opening net-30 vendor accounts and paying consistently. Getting to a score strong enough for bank financing typically takes 12 months or more of clean payment history.

### Can I build business credit without using my personal credit?

Yes, and that's the point. With the right setup (EIN, registered entity, business bank account) and vendor accounts that report to business bureaus, you can build business credit without tying it to your SSN or personal credit score. Early on, some lenders will still ask for a personal guarantee, but your business credit profile matters independently.

### What is a PAYDEX score and is it the same as a business credit score?

PAYDEX is Dun & Bradstreet's payment score, ranging from 1 to 100. It measures how promptly your business pays its bills. It's one of several business credit scores (Experian and Equifax have their own scoring models), but it's widely used by vendors and lenders, so it's worth understanding and monitoring specifically.

### Does opening a lot of vendor accounts hurt my business credit?

Not the way applying for multiple personal credit cards can hurt your personal score. Business credit inquiries are treated differently depending on the bureau and the type of account. That said, I wouldn't recommend opening ten accounts at once just to accelerate things. Start with 3 to 5, build history, then expand.

### What's the difference between business credit and personal credit for small business loans?

Personal credit determines whether you personally can be held responsible for the debt if your business can't pay. Business credit reflects your business's own repayment history and financial credibility. Many small business lenders look at both. The better your business credit, the more you can rely on it instead of your personal score, which protects your personal finances if something goes wrong with the business.
