Understanding the true cost of a business loan before you sign is one of the most important steps any entrepreneur can take. Whether you are considering an SBA 7(a) loan, a bank term loan, or a merchant cash advance, knowing your monthly payment, total interest burden, and payoff timeline lets you compare offers on a level playing field and negotiate from a position of knowledge.

## How Business Loan Interest Works

Traditional business loans use an **amortizing** structure, each monthly payment covers both interest and a portion of the principal. In early months, most of your payment goes toward interest; as the balance falls, more goes toward principal. This is why the total interest you pay depends heavily on your loan term: a longer term lowers monthly payments but dramatically increases what you pay over the life of the loan.

The key variables to understand are:

- **Principal (P):** The amount borrowed.
- **Annual interest rate (APR):** Expressed as a yearly percentage; your lender divides this by 12 to get the monthly rate.
- **Term (n):** The number of monthly payments, 36 months (3 years) is common for equipment and working capital; SBA loans often run 7–10 years.
- **Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR):** Your monthly revenue divided by your monthly loan payment. Lenders typically require 1.25× or higher.

## Factor Rates vs. APR: A Critical Distinction

Merchant cash advances (MCAs) and some online short-term lenders advertise a **factor rate** instead of an APR. A factor rate of 1.35 means you repay $1.35 for every $1.00 borrowed, sounds simple, but the actual annualized cost depends on how quickly you repay. On a 6-month repayment schedule, a 1.35 factor rate can translate to an APR well above 60%. On a 12-month schedule it is lower, but still far above most bank rates.

## How to Use These Results

Once you have your monthly payment figure, run it against your current cash flow. A general rule of thumb: your total monthly debt payments (all business loans combined) should not exceed **10–15% of gross monthly revenue** for a stable business. Higher ratios are manageable for fast-growing businesses with predictable revenue, but increase risk during slow seasons.

If the total interest figure looks high, consider making extra principal payments when cash flow allows, even one extra payment per year on a 5-year loan can cut months off the term and save hundreds in interest.
