If you're running a business and your business income lands in your personal checking account, you're working with a time bomb. It's one of the most common mistakes small business owners make -- and one of the most fixable.

Here's why it matters and how to fix it today.

## Why Separation Matters

**Legal protection.** If your business is an LLC or corporation, commingling funds can "pierce the corporate veil" -- meaning courts can hold you personally liable for business debts. The whole point of an LLC is that protection, and you lose it when you mix money.

**Tax accuracy.** When everything flows through one account, separating deductible business expenses from personal ones becomes a nightmare. You'll either miss deductions (costing money) or deduct personal expenses (inviting an audit).

**Cash flow visibility.** You can't see how your business is actually performing if personal expenses are muddying the picture.

## Step 1: Open a Business Checking Account

This is non-negotiable. Most banks offer free or low-fee business checking accounts for sole proprietors and LLCs. You'll need:

- Your EIN (Employer Identification Number) -- get one free at irs.gov in minutes
- Business formation documents (if you have an LLC)
- Your own ID

Recommended options: Relay, Bluevine, Mercury, or your local credit union. Chase, Bank of America, and Wells Fargo all offer business checking with varying fee structures.

## Step 2: Get a Business Credit Card

A business credit card creates a clean paper trail for expenses, builds your business credit score, and separates business charges automatically. Don't use your personal card for business purchases.

## Step 3: Pay Yourself a Salary or Owner's Draw

If you're an LLC or sole proprietor, transfer money from business to personal through regular "owner's draws." If you have an S-corp, pay yourself a reasonable salary. Document these transfers.

## Step 4: Use Accounting Software

QuickBooks Online, Wave (free), or FreshBooks make it easy to connect your business accounts, categorize transactions, and see your financial picture clearly. Do this from day one -- cleaning up a year of mixed transactions is miserable.

## What Happens If You Don't

The IRS loves audit triggers. One of the biggest? Business income and expenses that make no sense relative to your bank activity. Commingled accounts make your books messy and your tax situation murky.

Clean books aren't bureaucracy. They're how you protect yourself, understand your business, and make better decisions.
