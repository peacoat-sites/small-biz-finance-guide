Your Google Business Profile (the platform Google still commonly calls "Google My Business") is often the first thing a potential customer sees before they ever reach your website. Getting it right matters more than most owners realize, and the good news is that most of your competitors are doing it badly.

The core optimization moves aren't complicated: complete every field, choose the right primary category, post consistently, and respond to every review. That's the skeleton. The rest of this article fills in the muscle.

## Your Primary Category Is Doing More Work Than You Think

Most owners pick a category once during setup and forget about it. That's a mistake. Google uses your primary category as the main signal for which searches to show you in. If you run a coffee shop that also does catering, "Coffee Shop" should be your primary and "Caterer" can be a secondary. Flip that and you'll rank worse for the thing you do most.

You can have up to nine secondary categories (ten total including your primary), and you should use them thoughtfully. Don't pad with loosely related categories hoping for extra reach. Google is good at detecting that, and it can dilute your relevance for the searches that actually pay.

One thing the top search results often skip: your category also controls which special features appear on your profile. Restaurants get a menu link. Service businesses get a booking button. Hotels get star ratings displayed differently. If you're not seeing a feature you expect, check whether your category is the reason.

## Fill Out Everything. No, Really, Everything.

A half-complete profile sends a quiet signal that the business might not be active. Google rewards completeness because it means the platform can answer more user questions without sending people elsewhere.

Work through this in order:

1. **Business name** exactly as it appears on your signage. Don't stuff keywords in here. Google's guidelines prohibit it and it can get your listing suspended.
2. **Address or service area.** If you serve customers at their location (plumbers, dog walkers, mobile notaries), set a service area instead of a street address. You can hide your address if you don't have a public-facing location.
3. **Phone number** with a local area code where possible. Toll-free numbers work but local numbers perform better in local search.
4. **Website URL.** Link to the most specific page you can. A landing page for the city you're targeting beats your homepage.
5. **Hours.** Keep these accurate. Wrong hours are one of the most common complaints left in negative reviews.
6. **Business description.** 750 characters. Use it. Describe what you do, who you serve, and what makes you different. Don't keyword-stuff, but write naturally about your actual services.
7. **Products and services.** Add them individually with descriptions and prices where relevant. These appear directly on your profile and can answer purchase-intent questions before the customer has to call.
8. **Attributes.** These are the small checkboxes (women-led, veteran-owned, free Wi-Fi, outdoor seating). They show up in filters and help you match searches from people who care about those things.

## Photos: The Part Most Businesses Under-Invest In

Profiles with photos see more direction requests and website visits than those without, and that's not surprising. People want to see the place, the product, the team.

Upload at least: a cover photo, a logo, the interior, the exterior (from the street so customers can find you), your most popular product or service, and your team. Use actual photos, not stock images. Google can detect stock images and so can customers.

One thing worth knowing: Google lets customers upload photos to your profile too, and you can't remove most of them (you can flag truly inappropriate ones for review). The best defense is flooding your profile with high-quality owner-uploaded images so the crummy customer photos don't dominate.

## The Review Engine

Reviews affect your local ranking, full stop. The quantity, the recency, the rating, and whether the owner responds all factor in. The [IRS small business tax center](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed) won't help you here, but [SCORE's small business resources](https://www.score.org/) have solid guides on building customer loyalty systems that can feed your review funnel naturally.

Don't buy reviews. Google's fraud detection has gotten quite good, and a sudden influx of generic five-star reviews from accounts with no history can get your profile suspended. Getting suspended mid-quarter is worse than having fewer reviews.

What works instead: ask every satisfied customer. Have a short review link ready (Google generates a direct review URL from your profile dashboard). Text or email it after a job is complete. The ask matters more than the channel.

When someone leaves a negative review, respond within 48 hours, stay calm, don't argue, and offer to make it right offline. A graceful response to a bad review often does more for your reputation than five extra four-star reviews.

## Google Posts: Use Them or Lose Them

Google Posts appear directly on your profile in search results. Standard posts (offers, updates, events) expire after seven days. If you're not refreshing them, you have a blank slot where useful content should be.

What actually works in posts: specific offers with real deadlines, announcements of new services, or seasonal content. Vague "we love our customers" posts don't drive action. A post saying "book by November 30th for 10% off a full house cleaning" gives someone a reason to click.

You can schedule posts using tools like Hootsuite or Google's own Business Profile Manager. Set a weekly reminder if you're doing it manually.

## Tracking Whether Any of This Is Working

| Signal to Watch | Where to Find It | What to Do If It's Low |
|---|---|---|
| Search impressions | Business Profile Insights > Searches | Review category, add more secondary categories, improve description |
| Profile views | Business Profile Insights | Add more photos, increase post frequency |
| Direction requests | Business Profile Insights | Confirm your address is accurate, check map pin placement |
| Website clicks | Business Profile Insights | Optimize your URL to a targeted landing page |
| Review velocity | Reviews tab | Add a review-request step to your customer follow-up process |
| Review response rate | Reviews tab | Set a calendar reminder to check twice a week |

Google's Business Profile Insights dashboard is free and updated roughly every few days. Check it monthly at minimum.
