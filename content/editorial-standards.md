# How Small Biz Finance Guide Is Written

Small Biz Finance Guide covers the money side of running a small business: forming an entity, taxes, financing, cash flow and bookkeeping. This page explains, plainly, how the articles are made.

## How articles are made

Articles on Small Biz Finance Guide are drafted with AI writing tools. Each draft starts from public reference sources on the topic, including those listed below, and from recent news when a topic is changing.

Before an article is published, a second automated pass reviews it and removes or rewrites anything it cannot stand behind: statistics, prices or dates it is not confident are correct, studies or organizations that may be misattributed, and personal stories presented as if they happened to the writer.

Articles are not reviewed by a CPA, enrolled agent or attorney before publication. Use them as background for a conversation with one, not as a substitute.

The site owner audits published articles in batches, merging pages that overlap, deleting weak ones and correcting errors.

## Who writes here

The bylines on Small Biz Finance Guide (Amanda Pierce, David Kim, Michael Torres, Rachel Green, Sarah Johnson) are pen names, one for each area of coverage. They are not real individuals, they hold no professional licenses, and their profile images are illustrations, not photographs of real people.

## Sources we rely on

- [U.S. Small Business Administration](https://www.sba.gov/): Financing and business guidance
- [Internal Revenue Service](https://www.irs.gov/): Business tax rules
- [SCORE](https://www.score.org/): Free small-business mentoring
- [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/): Lending and finance

Where an article depends on a specific figure, rule or study, it links to or names the source so you can check it.

## Limits of this site

Tax rules, state filing fees and loan program terms change, and many depend on your state and situation. Use our figures and calculators as starting points, confirm current rules with the IRS, your state and the SBA, and talk to a CPA, enrolled agent or business attorney before acting on a tax or legal decision. SCORE mentors and Small Business Development Centers offer free or low-cost counseling.

## Corrections

If you find an error, please report it through the [contact page](/contact/). Reports go directly to the site owner, and confirmed errors are corrected in the article.

## How the site makes money

Small Biz Finance Guide may earn money from display advertising and from affiliate links, including Amazon links (as an Amazon Associate, the site earns from qualifying purchases). Advertisers and affiliate partners do not see or approve articles before they are published.
